How this works — the calculation & core
Why the index moves "randomly" in the last 15 minutes — and what actually drives it

You've seen it: Sensex drops 2000 pts (or Nifty jumps 200) between 15:15–15:30 while no stock moves that much. That's the tell — it's not the cash basket, it's option-expiry mechanics. On/near expiry, the index is dragged toward the strike where option writers lose least (Max Pain / highest OI), because writers and dealers hedge hard into the close and the auction concentrates those flows. The index derivative pins to the level the options "want," and the cash index follows — even though the underlying stocks barely move.

Closing Auction Session (CAS): the close is set by a short call-auction near 15:30 — orders pooled, one equilibrium price struck. Big desks concentrate closing orders here, so the print lands where the option positioning pulls it.

The core engine = MAX PAIN. For every strike we sum how much the option writers (sellers) would lose if price expired there — call writers pay when price is above their strike, put writers pay when it's below. The strike where writers lose the LEAST (buyers lose most) is Max Pain. Because writers are the big, hedged money, price gravitates toward Max Pain into expiry/close — they defend it.

  • Max Pain vs Spot → the pull: above spot = drift up, below = drift down, equal = pinned.
  • Call Wall (max call OI) = upside cap · Put Wall (max put OI) = downside floor. The close usually stays between them.
  • Gamma regime (dealer positioning): above the gamma flip dealers suppress moves → pin/range; below it they amplify → a wall-break can trend hard into the close.
  • PCR: high = put-writing (support), low = call-writing (cap).

How to track/trace: watch how OI shifts through the day (the walls migrate), and where spot sits vs Max Pain at ~3 PM. The prediction is captured near the close each day and scored against the actual close → the honest hit-rate above.
